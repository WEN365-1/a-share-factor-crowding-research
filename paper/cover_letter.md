## What the paper finds

Lou and Polk (2022) infer arbitrage activity from the return comovement of the stocks a
factor trades, and show that crowded US factors subsequently decay. Their measure is
indirect because positions are unobserved in their setting, so crowding is read off second moments.

Public margin-financing disclosures in China's A-share market make a balance-based financing
proxy observable. We compare it with
residual-return comovement for six style factors over 2015--2025. The margin-balance proxy is
conditionally negatively associated with pooled subsequent returns after controls for factor
momentum, balance levels, two-leg coverage, and factor fixed effects. The association is
concentrated in the low-side leg, but is not uniformly significant across factors or
subsamples. Purchase flows add no independent content in the reported horse races, while
Shanghai net flows retain content only at six months.

Comomentum produces more heterogeneous evidence. A closer weekly Lou--Polk analogue is
negatively associated with 12-month compounded momentum returns, but the result is sensitive
to construction and does not survive the strictest multiple-testing corrections. Pooled
long-horizon skewness results likewise do not generalise to momentum alone or to other risk
outcomes. Neither proxy is uniformly predictive, and the diagnostics do not validate either
as a measure of total crowded positions.

The two measures correlate weakly and with inconsistent signs across factors. We therefore
present a measurement comparison, not an account-level holdings measure, a causal estimate,
or a claim that one proxy has been directly validated by the other.

## Why this is not a single-country replication

We are aware that *Finance Research Letters* does not consider single-country replications
of well-established results, and we want to be explicit that this is not one.

The question is not whether a US finding reproduces in China, but whether two measurement
strategies remain interchangeable when an observable financing-balance proxy is available.
A return-correlation proxy and a balance-based proxy can disagree for reasons a single-measure
design cannot detect. Xing, Luo and Li (2026) construct a turnover-based crowding index for
a single factor in China; our measures are return-correlation and balance-based, span six
factors, and are compared within the same market. We position our study as a measurement
contribution that happens to use Chinese data, not as a single-country replication.

Trailing-window crowding measures sampled monthly create overlapping observations. We therefore
use a pooled circular-shift procedure that preserves the controls, sample, estimator, and HAC
lag length when re-estimating each shifted specification. This is an inferential design choice,
not evidence of a universal crowding effect; all economic results remain associational.
