## What the paper finds

Lou and Polk (2022) infer arbitrage activity from the return comovement of the stocks a
factor trades, and show that crowded US factors subsequently decay. Their measure is
indirect by necessity: positions are unobserved, so crowding is read off second moments.

China's A-share market makes a balance-based financing proxy observable. We compare it with
an indirect return-correlation proxy for six style factors over 2015--2025, and the two
produce different predictive results. The return-correlation proxy has no robust association
with subsequent factor returns under pooled circular-shift permutation inference. The
margin-balance proxy is conditionally negatively associated with subsequent returns after
controls for factor momentum, balance levels, coverage, and factor fixed effects. The
association is strongest in the primary pooled specifications, while subsample and window
results show meaningful sensitivity. We present the result as a measurement comparison, not
as an account-level holdings measure or a causal estimate.

The two measures correlate weakly and with inconsistent signs across factors, which is why
we report them side by side rather than choosing between them.

## Why this is not a single-country replication

We are aware that *Finance Research Letters* does not consider single-country replications
of well-established results, and we want to be explicit that this is not one.

The question is not whether a US finding reproduces in China, but whether two measurement
strategies remain interchangeable when an observable financing-balance proxy is available.
A return-correlation proxy and a balance-based proxy can disagree for reasons a single-measure
design cannot detect. Xing, Luo and Li (2026) construct a turnover-based crowding index for
a single factor in China; our measures are return-correlation and balance-based, span six
factors, and are compared within the same market. We read the paper as a measurement
contribution that happens to use Chinese data, not as a single-country replication.

The methodological result is also broader than the application: trailing-window crowding
measures sampled monthly create overlapping observations, so the paper uses a pooled
circular-shift procedure that re-estimates the observed specification for each shift. We
report this as an inferential design contribution, while keeping the economic results
associational.
