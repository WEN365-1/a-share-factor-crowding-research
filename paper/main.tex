\title{Measuring Factor Crowding When Leverage Is Observable:
       Evidence from China's A-Share Market}

\author[tongji]{Jiaheng Wen\corref{cor}}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author.}
\ead{2790443124@qq.com}
\address[tongji]{Tongji University}

\begin{abstract}
Factor crowding is difficult to measure because holdings are rarely observed and return-based proxies may capture several forms of common movement. We investigate whether two proxies identify the same predictive object when leverage is observable. Using six style factors in China's A-share market from 2015--2025, we compare residual-return comovement with a margin-financing-balance proxy. Within-factor comovement between the two measures is limited. Comomentum is not robustly associated with subsequent factor returns; its one-month volatility diagnostic is directionally consistent but the broader second-moment evidence is individually insignificant. The margin-balance proxy is conditionally negatively associated with subsequent factor returns after controlling for factor momentum, balance levels, coverage, and factor fixed effects. In the main six-month specification, a one-standard-deviation increase is associated with approximately a four-percentage-point lower horizon-sum factor return. The association is concentrated in the low-side leg: that leg's own financing intensity predicts its own decay, while the long-side leg contributes little, the effect does not depend on comomentum agreement, and flow-based measures of financing intensity tell the same story. Because rolling formation windows overlap, we use pooled circular-shift permutation inference alongside HAC standard errors. The results support a measurement distinction: return comovement and observable leveraged financing need not be interchangeable measures of factor crowding, and the evidence is associational rather than causal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A factor that attracts too much capital should eventually stop working: with limited
arbitrage, capital flows into a strategy until the mispricing is gone, and the trades most
exposed to a reversal are the crowded ones
\citep{shleifer1997limits,delong1990noise,stein2009presidential,barberis2003style}. What is
unsettled is measurement: positions are unobserved, and returns are contaminated by the
price pressure crowding itself creates.

\citet{lou2022comomentum} propose a way around this. If many arbitrageurs hold the same
stocks, those stocks should comove beyond what common factors explain. Their
\emph{comomentum} measure, the average pairwise correlation of three-factor residual
returns within the stocks a momentum strategy trades, forecasts lower momentum returns,
higher volatility and more negative skewness. It is indirect by construction: it infers
positioning from second moments.

We ask whether that inference survives where an alternative is observable. China's A-share
market combines a distinct margin-financing institution with tighter short-sale eligibility
than the setting in which comomentum was developed. We therefore compare the return-
correlation proxy with a balance-based proxy.

The comparison itself is the contribution: a correlation-based and a balance-based proxy
can give different predictive answers, complicating any single-proxy interpretation.
We construct both for six style factors over 2015--2025 on the full A-share cross-section,
including delisted firms. The two measures have limited linear comovement and disagree on
predictability: the margin-balance proxy is negatively associated with subsequent factor
returns, whereas comomentum is not robustly predictive.

Three concerns shape the design. The first is a mechanical component: a balance-based
change measure contains information about the balance level, which correlates with market
capitalisation and factor membership. We control for the level throughout; comparing
specifications A and C diagnoses, but does not eliminate, this component.

The second concerns factor momentum: factors that performed well may attract financing.
We separate the two by orthogonalising the proxy against the factor's past 1-, 6- and
12-month returns, including both simultaneously, and estimating factor-specific regressions.
Factor fixed effects identify the pooled coefficient from within-factor variation.

The third concerns inference. With a twelve-month formation window sampled monthly, adjacent
observations share eleven twelfths of their data, so we report pooled circular-shift
permutation p-values with the same estimator used for the observed statistic.

The paper contributes to two literatures. To the literature on factor crowding and decay
\citep{mclean2016does,beck2016will,lou2022comomentum}, it adds a measurement comparison
under a different arbitrage regime. The return-correlation and financing-balance proxies
span six factors and are evaluated within the same market. To the literature on China's
margin trading \citep{bian2026drivers,xing2026pmo}, it adds a factor-level association
between financing balances and subsequent factor returns. The contribution is the
measurement comparison and its inferential design, not a claim that either proxy directly
observes total investor positions.

\section{Data and methodology}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Data}

We use the full A-share cross-section from January 2015 to December 2025. Daily prices,
trading volumes, turnover, and point-in-time valuation ratios are from Baostock; the
universe includes delisted firms. The monthly panel covers 5{,}247 of the 5{,}418 stocks in
the cleaned universe over 2{,}674 trading days. Individual-stock margin-financing balances,
covering the designated margin-eligible list, are from the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges.

We impose a next-trading-day availability rule: the month-end signal uses only balances
available by that date; an audit confirms no observation post-dates its signal. The valuation ratios are treated as point-in-time fields based on a
40-stock audit documented in the replication materials.

We exclude stocks that are suspended, have been listed fewer than 180 days, or are flagged
ST, and we drop financial firms using the available industry classification (a
current-snapshot classification applied to the full sample). Within the monthly
cross-section we additionally drop stock-months with more than ten non-trading days,
and we apply the same rule when forming the decile legs.
The margin-eligible list expands materially over the sample, so we construct factor
portfolios on the full cross-section and compute the margin-balance proxy only over
constituent stocks, with coverage controls.

Style factors are constructed monthly. Value (VAL) is book-to-market; size (SMB) is
negative log float market capitalisation; momentum (MOM) is the compounded return over
months $t-12$ to $t-1$; quality (QMJ) is implied return on equity, price-to-book divided by
trailing price-to-earnings; low volatility (LOWVOL) is negative realised volatility over
the prior 60 trading days; turnover (TURN) is negative mean turnover over the prior 20
days. Decile portfolios are float-capitalisation-weighted, and factor returns are
top-decile minus bottom-decile returns, with the long side predicted to earn higher
returns.

\subsection{Two measures of crowding}

\paragraph{Indirect: comomentum.}
Following \citet{lou2022comomentum}, for each factor $f$ and month $t$ we take the long and short
deciles formed on $f$'s exposure, regress each constituent's daily returns over the
trailing 250 trading days on a three-factor benchmark, and compute the average pairwise
correlation of residual returns within each leg; the measure is the average across the two
legs. Because each leg is capped at 100 randomly selected stocks when necessary, it is a
sampled pairwise-correlation measure; the seed is fixed for replication.

\paragraph{Margin-financing-balance proxy.}
For each constituent we compute the monthly change in the publicly reported margin-financing--
balance scaled by float market capitalisation, $\Delta MB_{i,t}/MV_{i,t}$, and aggregate to
the leg level using float-capitalisation weights. The measure is the long-leg aggregate minus
the short-leg aggregate. We also retain the long-leg aggregate, the margin level, and
coverage. Under exchange disclosure rules, the previous trading day's information is
published before the next session, so we use the next trading day as a conservative
availability date. Coverage is uneven: the size factor's long leg has mean margin coverage
of 0.26 (61 per cent of months below 0.30), against 0.82--0.93 elsewhere. We therefore
control for coverage and re-estimate results under coverage floors. This
proxy is more directly observed than comomentum, but it is not a pure financing flow, an
account-level position, or a causal measure of crowding.

\subsection{Empirical design}

For each measure $C_{f,t}$ and horizon $h \in \{1,3,6,12\}$ we estimate
\begin{equation}
R^{f}_{t \to t+h} \;=\; \alpha_f \;+\; \beta\, C_{f,t} \;+\; \gamma' X_{f,t} \;+\; \varepsilon_{f,t},
\end{equation}
where $R^{f}_{t\to t+h}$ is the horizon sum of the next $h$ monthly long-short returns,
starting in $t+1$, $\alpha_f$ is a
factor fixed effect, and $X$ collects the factor's own past 1-, 6-, and 12-month returns,
the margin level, and coverage. Standard errors are Newey-West with
six lags. Because the six factors share a market shock within a month, factor-level
clustering is unreliable with so few clusters.

Our central concern is that $\beta$ reflects factor momentum rather than crowding. We
address it three ways: by orthogonalising $C_{f,t}$ against the factor's own past 1-,
6-, and 12-month returns and re-running the regression on the residual; by including
crowding and past returns simultaneously; and by estimating the regression separately for
each factor.

For each permutation we shift the measure
within factor and re-estimate the same pooled specification, preserving factor fixed effects,
controls, the sample, and the HAC procedure. We use the finite-permutation correction
$(1+B_{\geq})/(1+B)$ for the reported $p$-values and treat them as the primary inferential
quantities; HAC $p$-values are shown for comparison. We validate the correction on synthetic
panels with no crowding effect by construction, and since a null result is only informative
if the design has power, we compute minimum detectable effects by block bootstrap; both
checks are in the replication materials.

The primary evidence family is the two proxies crossed with Layers 1 and 3 and horizons
$h\in\{1,3,6\}$; horizon 12 and the remaining analyses are auxiliary.

\subsection{Margin-balance proxy predicts decay; comomentum does not}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the primary coefficients and permutation $p$-values; HAC
$t$-statistics are retained in the replication outputs. Comomentum
has no robust predictive association with subsequent factor returns in this sample and design:
permutation $p$-values run from $0.36$ to $0.95$, and the point estimates change sign across
horizons. The margin-balance proxy, by contrast, is negatively associated with subsequent
returns.

The pattern across specifications is informative. With no controls (column A) the margin-balance
coefficient is negative but imprecise. Adding the factor's own past 1-, 6- and 12-month
returns (column B) increases its magnitude; at $h=6$, $\beta$ is about $-0.034$ (permutation
$p=0.010$). Controlling for the margin level (C) and coverage (D) leaves the estimates
essentially unchanged, diagnosing but not eliminating concerns about the level component of
balance changes.

The magnitude is economically material: in specification D at $h=6$,
a one-standard-deviation increase in the margin-balance proxy accompanies approximately a
$4.0$ percentage point lower horizon-sum long-short return over six months ($3.3$ points
per within-factor standard deviation). This is a
conditional predictive association, not an estimate of a causal effect.

\subsection{The signal is in the low-side leg}

China's margin system finances long positions almost exclusively, so we ask which leg
carries the association. In Table~\ref{tab:legs}, Panel A regresses each leg's horizon-sum
return on the total margin-balance measure with specification-D controls and sample, so the
long-short coefficient equals the long-leg coefficient minus the short-leg coefficient. At
$h=6$ the long leg contributes nothing ($\beta=0.0005$, permutation $p=0.97$) while the
short leg carries the entire effect ($\beta=0.0406$, $p=0.010$). Panel B pairs each leg with
its own financing measure: the low-side leg's financing intensity predicts its own decay
strongly ($\beta=-0.0517$ at $h=6$, $p=0.010$); the long-side leg shows the same sign but
is insignificant ($\beta=-0.0162$, $p=0.14$). The association reflects low-side financing
intensity rather than a fragile spread artifact. Comomentum shows no leg-level pattern.

\subsection{Flows versus stocks}

The balance-change measure is a stock concept: it captures where financing stands. Daily
financing purchases and repayments are also disclosed, so we construct a flow-based
variant: the monthly sum of financing purchases scaled by float market capitalisation,
aggregated as before, with its trailing twelve-month mean as a level control. Net flows,
purchases less repayments, are available for Shanghai only. Table~\ref{tab:flow} shows that
both flow measures are negatively associated with subsequent factor returns; the Shanghai
net-flow measure is significant at all four horizons (permutation $p=0.016$--$0.019$). When
flow and balance-change measures compete in the same specification, the balance-change
measure remains significant at every horizon, while purchase flows retain independent
content at one month ($p=0.016$) and net flows at six months ($p=0.034$). The two
margin-based measures are only moderately correlated ($0.44$--$0.50$), so stocks and flows
capture partly distinct signals.

\subsection{The return-correlation proxy is not merely a failed implementation}

The obvious objection is that our implementation of comomentum fails, rather than the
proxy itself. \citet{lou2022comomentum} also document that comomentum
forecasts higher realised volatility and more negative skewness. Table~\ref{tab:vol} shows
some directional consistency in China: the one-month volatility coefficient has permutation
$p = 0.059$, while the skewness results are not individually significant. This is supportive
diagnostic evidence, not a full replication.

The margin-balance proxy is negatively associated with returns and has no reliable relation
to the second moments. The two proxies therefore need not be interchangeable: comomentum
tracks residual-return comovement, whereas the margin-balance proxy tracks a balance-based
financing signal.

\subsection{Robustness and scope of the null result}

Table~\ref{tab:rob} splits the sample, changes the weighting, and varies the construction
window. The margin-balance association remains detectable at the $0.10$ level in 2015--2018
($p=0.194$) but not in 2019--2021 ($p=0.111$) or 2022--2025 ($p=0.149$), although all three
subsample estimates are negative. It survives equal weighting ($p=0.008$) and weakens when
the balance change is measured over six or twenty-four months. Restricting the measure to
either leg alone removes the one-month association ($p=0.67$, $0.22$), consistent with the
leg decomposition. These results show directional and
design sensitivity; they should not be described as universal robustness.

Further checks support the main specification. Compounding returns leaves the coefficient
slightly larger ($-0.042$ at $h=6$, $p=0.010$). Excluding the size factor, whose margin
coverage is thinnest, strengthens the association ($-0.049$, $p=0.010$), and coverage
floors of $0.30$ or $0.50$ leave it intact ($-0.046$ at $h=6$; $p=0.022$, $0.025$). The
margin signal does not depend on comomentum agreement: coefficients in months above and
below the factor-specific comomentum median are similar ($-0.036$ and $-0.035$ at $h=6$),
and the interaction is insignificant ($p=0.20$). The two proxies act as independent signals
rather than complementary ones.

Because one result is a null, its informativeness matters: minimum detectable effects
from the block bootstrap are about $11$--$14$ per cent annualised for comomentum. The null
is informative relative to effects above this threshold.

\section{Conclusion}

Two proxies for factor crowding, applied to the same factors in the same market, give
substantially different predictive results. The return-correlation proxy has no robust
association with subsequent factor returns in this sample and design. The margin-balance
proxy is negatively associated with subsequent returns after controls for factor momentum,
the balance level, coverage, factor fixed effects, and pooled circular-shift permutation
inference. The association is concentrated in the low-side leg, whose own financing
intensity predicts its own decay, it does not depend on comomentum agreement, and
flow-based financing measures echo the balance-based result. The result
is conditional and associational, not a causal estimate or an account-level holdings result.

The evidence supports a measurement distinction. Comomentum provides a residual-return
comovement signal with limited directional evidence in the second-moment diagnostics, while
the margin-balance proxy provides the stronger return-predictive association. The two proxies
should therefore be reported side by side rather than treated as interchangeable. The
subsample and window results show that the association is not universal, and the tail
analysis does not establish disproportionate crash risk.

Trailing-window measures sampled monthly create overlapping observations; the pooled
circular-shift procedure preserves each factor's measure persistence and is the primary
inferential procedure here. Future
work should use historical industry classifications, account- or position-level data, and
quasi-experimental variation in margin eligibility or disclosure.
