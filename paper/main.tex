\title{Measuring Factor Crowding When Leverage Is Observable:
       Evidence from China's A-Share Market}

\author[tongji]{Jiaheng Wen\corref{cor}\ead{2790443124@qq.com}}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author.}
\address[tongji]{Tongji University}

\begin{abstract}
Factor crowding is difficult to measure because holdings are rarely observed and return-based proxies may capture several forms of common movement. We investigate whether two proxies identify the same predictive object when leverage is observable. Using six style factors in China's A-share market from 2015--2025, we compare residual-return comovement with a margin-financing-balance proxy. Within-factor comovement between the two measures is limited. Comomentum is not robustly associated with subsequent factor returns; its one-month volatility diagnostic is directionally consistent but the broader second-moment evidence is exploratory. The margin-balance proxy is conditionally negatively associated with subsequent factor returns after controlling for factor momentum, balance levels, coverage, and factor fixed effects. In the main six-month specification, a one-standard-deviation increase is associated with approximately a four-percentage-point lower horizon-sum factor return. Because rolling formation windows overlap, we use pooled circular-shift permutation inference alongside HAC standard errors. The results support a measurement distinction: return comovement and observable leveraged financing need not be interchangeable measures of factor crowding, and the evidence is associational rather than causal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A factor that attracts too much capital should, in the end, stop working. Arbitrage is
limited, so capital flows into a strategy until the mispricing it exploits is gone, and the
trades most exposed to a reversal are the crowded ones
\citep{shleifer1997limits,delong1990noise,stein2009presidential,barberis2003style}. What is
not settled is the measurement: positions are unobserved, and the returns that would reveal
them are contaminated by the price pressure crowding is supposed to create.

\citet{lou2022comomentum} propose a way around this. If many arbitrageurs hold the same
stocks, those stocks should comove beyond what common factors explain. Their
\emph{comomentum} measure, the average pairwise correlation of three-factor residual
returns within the stocks a momentum strategy trades, forecasts lower momentum returns,
higher volatility and more negative skewness. It is indirect by construction: it infers
positioning from second moments for want of anything better.

We ask whether that inference survives where an alternative is observable. China's A-share
market combines a distinct margin-financing institution with tighter short-sale eligibility
than the setting in which comomentum was developed. We therefore compare the return-
correlation proxy with a balance-based proxy rather than treating either one as a definitive
measure of crowding.

That comparison is the point. A correlation-based proxy and a balance-based proxy can
produce different predictive results, making the interpretation of any single proxy difficult.
We construct both for six style factors over 2015--2025 on the full A-share cross-section;
the initial universe includes delisted firms. The two measures have limited linear
comovement and give different answers about predictability: the margin-balance proxy is
negatively associated with subsequent factor returns, whereas comomentum is not robustly
predictive.

The first concerns a mechanical component of balance changes. A balance-based change
measure, $\Delta MB_{i,t}/MV_{i,t}$, contains information about the balance level, which is
related to market capitalisation and factor membership. We therefore control for the level
throughout. The comparison between specifications A and C is a diagnostic for this mechanical
component, not proof that all measurement error has been removed.

The second concerns factor momentum. Factors that performed well may attract financing,
so crowding and past factor returns can move together. We separate them by orthogonalising
the proxy against the factor's past 1-, 6- and 12-month returns, including both variables
simultaneously, and estimating factor-specific time-series regressions. Factor fixed effects
ensure that the pooled coefficient is identified from within-factor variation rather than
persistent differences in average crowding.

The third concerns inference. The formation window is twelve months but we sample it monthly,
so adjacent observations share eleven twelfths of their data. We therefore report pooled
circular-shift permutation p-values with the same estimator used for the observed statistic.

The paper contributes to two literatures. To the literature on factor crowding and decay
\citep{mclean2016does,beck2016will,lou2022comomentum}, it adds a measurement comparison
under a different arbitrage regime. The return-correlation and financing-balance proxies
span six factors and are evaluated within the same market. To the literature on China's
margin trading \citep{bian2026drivers,xing2026pmo}, it adds a factor-level association
between financing balances and subsequent factor returns. The contribution is the
measurement comparison and its inferential design, not a claim that either proxy directly
observes total investor positions.

\section{Data and methodology}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Data}

We use the full A-share cross-section from January 2015 to December 2025. Daily prices,
trading volumes, turnover, and point-in-time valuation ratios are from Baostock; the
universe includes delisted firms. The sample covers 5{,}247 stocks over 2{,}674 trading days.
Individual-stock margin-financing balances, covering the designated margin-eligible list, are
from the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges.

We treat the raw margin date as an occurrence date and impose a conservative next-trading-day
availability rule. The common month-end signal is formed only from balances available by that
date; the resulting audit has no observations available after the signal date. The valuation
ratios are treated as point-in-time fields based on the multi-stock audit documented in the
replication materials.

We exclude stocks that are suspended, have been listed fewer than 180 days, or are flagged
ST, and we drop financial firms using the available industry classification. Within the
monthly cross-section we additionally drop stock-months with more than ten non-trading days,
and we apply the same rule when forming the decile legs that define the crowding measures.
The margin-eligible list expands materially over the sample. We therefore construct factor
portfolios on the full cross-section rather than within the eligible pool, and compute the
margin-balance proxy only over constituent stocks, with coverage controls for the changing
eligible universe.

\subsection{Two measures of crowding}

\paragraph{Indirect: comomentum.}
Following \citet{lou2022comomentum}, for each factor $f$ and month $t$ we take the long and short
deciles formed on $f$'s exposure at $t$, regress each constituent's daily returns over the
trailing 250 trading days on a three-factor benchmark constructed from the cleaned universe,
and compute the average pairwise correlation of the residual returns within each leg. The
measure is the average across the two legs. It is a return-correlation proxy, not an
account-level holdings measure. Because each leg is capped at 100 randomly selected stocks
when necessary, it is also a sampled pairwise-correlation measure; the sampling seed is
fixed for replication.

\paragraph{Margin-financing-balance proxy.}
For each constituent we compute the monthly change in the publicly reported margin-financing--
balance scaled by float market capitalisation, $\Delta MB_{i,t}/MV_{i,t}$, and aggregate to
the leg level using float-capitalisation weights. The measure is the long-leg aggregate minus
the short-leg aggregate. We also retain the long leg separately, the aggregate margin level,
and the portfolio's margin coverage. The raw balance date is treated as an occurrence date;
under the exchange disclosure rules, the previous trading day's information is published
before the next trading session, so we use the next trading day as a conservative availability
date. This proxy is more directly observed than comomentum, but it is not a pure financing
flow, an account-level position, or a causal measure of crowding.

\subsection{Empirical design}

For each measure $C_{f,t}$ and horizon $h \in \{1,3,6,12\}$ we estimate
\begin{equation}
R^{f}_{t \to t+h} \;=\; \alpha_f \;+\; \beta\, C_{f,t} \;+\; \gamma' X_{f,t} \;+\; \varepsilon_{f,t},
\end{equation}
where $R^{f}_{t\to t+h}$ is the horizon sum of the next $h$ monthly long-short returns,
starting in $t+1$ (we report compound-return sensitivity separately), $\alpha_f$ is a
factor fixed effect, and $X$ collects the factor's own past 1-, 6-, and 12-month returns,
the margin level, and the portfolio's margin coverage. Standard errors are Newey-West with
six lags. Because the six factors share a market shock within a month, factor-level clustering
is not reliable with so few clusters, and we do not rely on it.

Our central concern is that $\beta$ reflects factor momentum rather than crowding. We
address it three ways: by orthogonalising $C_{f,t}$ against the factor's own past 1-,
6-, and 12-month returns and re-running the regression on the residual; by including
crowding and past returns simultaneously; and by estimating the regression separately for
each factor, which asks whether the sign is common or driven by one factor.

Finally, because the formation window is twelve months but sampling is monthly, adjacent
observations share eleven twelfths of their data. For each permutation we shift the measure
within factor and re-estimate the same pooled specification, preserving factor fixed effects,
controls, the sample, and the HAC procedure. We use the finite-permutation correction
$(1+B_{\geq})/(1+B)$ for the reported $p$-values and treat them as the primary inferential
quantities; HAC $p$-values are shown for comparison.

We validate the correction rather than assert it. On synthetic panels with no crowding
effect by construction, across three independent draws the revised pooled permutation
procedure provides a calibration check; the complete calibration output and seeds are in the
replication materials. Since a null result is only informative if the design has power, we
also compute minimum detectable effects by block bootstrap, injecting known effects into the
actual panel and recording the rejection rate at the permutation critical value.

The primary evidence family is the two proxies crossed with Layers 1 and 3 and horizons
$h\in\{1,3,6\}$. Horizon 12, factor-specific regressions, tail analysis, second-moment
diagnostics, and robustness splits are auxiliary. We use finite pooled circular-shift
permutation p-values for the primary family and report HAC statistics for comparison.

\subsection{The two measures of crowding are only weakly related}

The two measures have limited linear comovement, although this alone does not establish that
they identify distinct economic objects. Table~\ref{tab:corr} reports the within-factor
correlation between comomentum and the margin-balance proxy. It ranges from $-0.29$ for
turnover to $+0.27$ for size, and is near zero for low-volatility. Pooling the six factors
and removing factor-specific means leaves $-0.11$. These correlations are descriptive and
may also reflect timing, coverage, nonlinear dependence, or sampling noise.

\subsection{Margin-balance proxy predicts decay; comomentum does not}

The primary coefficients and finite permutation $p$-values are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:main}; HAC $t$-statistics are retained in the replication outputs. Comomentum
has no robust predictive association with subsequent factor returns in this sample and design:
permutation $p$-values run from $0.30$ to $0.95$, and the point estimates change sign across
horizons. The margin-balance proxy, by contrast, is negatively associated with subsequent
returns, with the effect remaining after controls are added.

The pattern across specifications is informative. With no controls (column A) the margin-balance
coefficient is negative but imprecisely estimated. Adding the factor's own past 1-, 6- and
12-month returns (column B) increases the coefficient's absolute magnitude; at $h=6$,
$\beta$ is about $-0.034$ and the permutation $p$-value is about $0.010$. Controlling for
the margin level (C) and coverage (D) leaves the estimates essentially unchanged. This
comparison diagnoses, but does not eliminate, concerns about the level component of balance
changes.

The magnitude is economically material in this descriptive sense: in specification D at $h=6$,
a one-standard-deviation increase in the margin-balance proxy accompanies approximately a
$4.0$ percentage point lower horizon-sum long-short return over six months. This is a
conditional predictive association, not an estimate of a causal effect.

\subsection{The return-correlation proxy is not merely a failed implementation}

The obvious objection to the previous section is that our implementation of comomentum
fails, rather than the proxy itself. \citet{lou2022comomentum} also document that comomentum
forecasts higher realised volatility and more negative skewness. Table~\ref{tab:vol} shows
some directional consistency in China: the one-month volatility coefficient has permutation
$p = 0.059$, while the skewness results are not individually significant under the revised
pooled permutation procedure. This is supportive diagnostic evidence, not a full replication
or proof that the proxy identifies account-level crowding.

The margin-balance proxy shows a different pattern: it is negatively associated with returns
and has no reliable relation to the second moments in the primary specifications. The two
proxies therefore need not be interchangeable. Comomentum tracks residual-return
comovement, whereas the margin-balance proxy tracks a balance-based financing signal.

\subsection{Robustness and scope of the null result}

Table~\ref{tab:rob} splits the sample, changes the weighting, and varies the construction
window. The margin-balance association is weak in 2015--2018 ($p=0.33$), not retained at the
$0.10$ level in 2019--2021 ($p=0.11$) or 2022--2025 ($p=0.15$), and remains detectable under
equal weighting ($p=0.008$). It weakens when the balance change is measured over six or
twenty-four months and for the long leg alone. These results show directional and design
sensitivity; they should not be described as universal robustness.

Because one of the two results is a null, its informativeness matters. Under the revised
pooled permutation design, minimum detectable effects from the block bootstrap are about
$11$--$14$ per cent annualised for comomentum across the reported horizons. The null is
informative relative to effects above this design-specific detection threshold; it does not
establish equivalence to zero.

\section{Conclusion}

Two proxies for factor crowding, applied to the same factors in the same market, give
substantially different predictive results. The return-correlation proxy has no robust
association with subsequent factor returns in this sample and design. The margin-balance
proxy is negatively associated with subsequent returns after controls for factor momentum,
the balance level, coverage, factor fixed effects, and pooled circular-shift permutation
inference. The result is conditional and associational, not a causal estimate or an
account-level holdings result.

The evidence supports a measurement distinction. Comomentum provides a residual-return
comovement signal with limited directional evidence in the second-moment diagnostics, while
the margin-balance proxy provides the stronger return-predictive association. The two proxies
should therefore be reported side by side rather than treated as interchangeable. The
subsample and window results show that the association is not universal, and the auxiliary
tail analysis does not establish disproportionate crash risk.

Trailing-window measures sampled monthly create overlapping observations. The pooled
circular-shift procedure preserves each factor's measure persistence and re-estimates the
same specification for every shift; it is the primary inferential procedure here. Future
work should use historical industry classifications, account- or position-level data, and
quasi-experimental variation in margin eligibility or disclosure.
